Problem 2
==========

A space agency wants to keep track of astronauts, the missions they participate in, and the spacecraft used for these missions.
-- Each astronaut has a unique ID, a name, and a nationality.
-- Each mission has a unique mission number, a name, and a destination.
-- The agency maintains a fleet of vehicles. Each spacecraft has a unique spacecraft ID, a model name, and a crew capacity.

An astronaut can participate in multiple missions, and a mission can include multiple astronauts. For every astronaut who participates 
in a mission, the agency records the role that the astronaut performs on that mission (such as Commander, Pilot, Engineer, or Scientist).

To execute a mission, a vehicle is required. Therefore, every mission must be assigned to exactly one spacecraft (total participation). 
However, a spacecraft can exist in the agency's fleet without ever being assigned to a mission (partial participation), such as when a vehicle 
is newly built or grounded for maintenance.

Problem 4
=========

A university physics department needs a database to track student enrollment, laboratory teams, lab sections, and experimental data from drag physics experiments.

Each student is identified by a unique StudentID and has a first name, last name, and grade. Students can be members of lab teams, and each lab team can have 
multiple students. Each lab team has one student leader, while a student may lead a lab team.

A lab section is identified by its catalog number, year, semester, and section, and it has a lab assistant and a Drag Experiment Week. Each lab section is 
associated with one lab team, while a lab team may participate in multiple lab sections.

Each lab section performs multiple drag-experiment runs, while each run belongs to one lab section. A DragExpRun is identified by a RunNumber and records the time, 
initial position, and distance of the experiment. Each drag-experiment run is conducted by one lab team, while a lab team can conduct multiple runs.
